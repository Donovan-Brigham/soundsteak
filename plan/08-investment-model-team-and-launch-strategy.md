## The Investment Model — Structure and Terms

### The Application Process

Applying to SoundSteak is not a form submission. It is a proof-of-existence process with two required elements:

**1. A verified live video.** The platform provides a recording mechanism — a real-time, platform-verified capture. Think Zoom-equivalent functionality built into the application flow. The system confirms the video is being recorded live, not uploaded from existing footage. Artists can optionally submit a higher-quality audio capture after the fact (self-recorded during the same session), but the platform-verified live video is the non-negotiable requirement. This is how SoundSteak confirms real artists with real music exist behind every application.

**2. Fan pledges.** At $1 each. The pledges cover pre-signing platform costs and prove that real people believe in the artist — not just the artist themselves. Hitting pledge goals requires building or activating a real audience, however small. Someone who has never played a show and has no community cannot hit them. That is the filter.

Pledge goals are tiered. The number and meaning of each goal will be worked out backward from what the platform's actual pre-signing costs are. Goal 1 is currently 50 pledges. Further goals and what they unlock or signal are to be determined as the cost model becomes clearer.

The pledge process also serves as onboarding: every person who pledges $1 creates a profile. The application is a growth mechanism, not just a selection mechanism. Every artist's campaign brings their network onto the platform before a contract has been signed.

The selection decision — who gets to the event and receives a contract — is fan-driven and data-driven. The label does not pick winners.

### The Two-Event Structure

The artist's journey from contract to release spans exactly two annual events. This is not a timeline — it is the architecture.

**Event One — The Contract and the IPO**
When the gong rings to open the show, two things happen simultaneously:
1. The artist is awarded their recording contract on stage
2. The single's ownership goes up for sale — the IPO is live

Investing begins the night the contract is signed. Not the night the single drops. That distinction is fundamental: investors fund the recording before it exists. They are backing the artist and the journey, not the finished product.

An LLC is formed for each single release at the point of signing. Investors are purchasing ownership in that LLC — a legal entity, not a token.

**Event Two — The Release**
Exactly one year later, at the next annual event, the artist performs their finished single for the first time. This is the graduation. Investors who backed the recording hear it live. The single is released to all platforms.

The funding threshold must be met within a defined window after Event One for recording to begin. Speed is by design. The platform does not sit on an open raise for months. If the threshold is not met in time, recording doesn't start and investors are returned their pledges. This is not a risk — it is a transparency mechanism. A raise that fills fast is itself a signal to the market.

### LLC Per Release

At signing, an LLC is formed for that single. This entity:
- Owns the recording
- Receives all earnings from that recording
- Has an investor cap table as its ownership structure
- Files taxes and issues 1099s automatically through the platform
- Has a pre-defined exit (buyout) clause written into its operating agreement

The platform handles all LLC formation. The artist does not navigate this alone. One of the concrete benefits of the SoundSteak path versus going independent is that the administrative infrastructure of being a musician is handled by default.

### Ownership Structure

**Platform fee** comes off the top first. This is non-negotiable — it funds operations and is disclosed in full before any investment is made. The artist's percentage and investor percentage are calculated on what remains after the platform fee.

**Artist ownership**: The artist decides how much of their share to retain. They can keep any percentage they choose — or give away all of it if that is their decision. They can also designate a portion of their share to a charity of their choice. That is documented in the contract and flows automatically through the distribution system.

**Investor ownership**: The remaining stake — whatever the artist has not retained — is the pool available for investment.

**The alignment principle**: This record label does what is in the best interest of the artist — because that is what is in the best interest of the SteakHolders, and in turn, SoundSteak. That alignment is not a policy. It is the structure.

### Pre-Orders — The Merch Machine Starts at Signing

Pre-orders for merchandise (and anything else the platform can offer) begin immediately after the contract is signed — before a shirt is designed, before anything is printed. The sequence is:

1. Contract signed at Event One
2. Artist designs the first piece of merch on the platform
3. Pre-orders go live on the artist's profile immediately — before production
4. SteakHolders pre-purchase and share the listing
5. When enough pre-orders exist to justify a print run, the run happens — zero inventory risk

Pre-orders are not tied to the single's release date. They are tied to the signing. This means pre-orders launch, run, and fulfill on their own timeline independent of when the music drops.

**Why this matters for delayed artists:** If an artist slips their release to a later event, the merch machine keeps running. New orders continue to open. The community's ongoing financial activity — buying, sharing, pre-ordering — keeps the relationship alive through the delay. A setback communicated transparently, with visible community support and active pre-orders, can actually deepen SteakHolder investment rather than erode it. The pre-order becomes a public vote of confidence. The delay becomes a chapter in the story, not the end of it.

**The fan as the marketing machine:** Every SteakHolder who shares a pre-order link is acting in their own financial interest. They don't need to be asked. The economic alignment turns passive supporters into an active, self-motivated distribution network — functioning simultaneously as manager, street team, PR firm, and marketing agency, all running on the same engine: their own stake in the outcome.

### Ongoing Investment — No Rounds

There is no founding round, no Series A, no secondary round. Investment in a release is a continuous, open opportunity for as long as the music is on the platform. The LLC earns indefinitely. New revenue streams open as the platform grows — sync placements years after release, new markets, new platforms, new opportunities the team hasn't anticipated yet. The platform's standing mandate is to find every possible way to maximize what a stake earns and to keep improving that number over time.

"Founding round" language is specifically avoided. It implies the window closes. This window doesn't close.

### First Come, First Served

No minimums. No maximums. One person can buy the entire available stake if they are fast enough to press the button. This is not a rule for the sake of simplicity — it is a philosophical statement about access. The fan who has $10 and acts in the first minute has the same shot as the investor with $10,000 who acts in the second minute.

This creates urgency. It rewards attention and conviction. It makes the IPO night feel like what it is: a once-in-a-year moment where early believers get in first.

### The Funding Threshold

A minimum funding goal is set before each raise opens. This represents the actual cost of recording the single — studio time, production, mixing, mastering, video, PR. Every line item is publicly disclosed. The platform knows the cost of everything because it has to — the funding goal must be defensible to every investor who reads it.

If the threshold is not met within the defined window: the project does not proceed. Investors receive full refunds. The artist is not penalized — they reapply for the next cycle. Threshold failure before recording begins protects everyone. It is far less disruptive than a project stalling mid-production.

### The Buyout

Every band agreement and every investor disclosure includes a clear, pre-defined buyout clause. Investors must know from the moment they invest that a buyout is a possible future event — not a surprise exit, but a transparent mechanism.

A buyout means a third party (a label, the artist, or any other buyer) purchases all outstanding SteakHolder ownership in the LLC at a defined formula. Investors receive their buyout payment. The LLC's ownership is consolidated under the buyer. The music moves on.

Investors should understand the buyout not as a risk but as a feature: it is one of the potential exit paths in an otherwise illiquid investment. A buyout at a premium is a return, not a loss.

### Terminology — Internal vs. Public

**"Cycle"** is the public-facing term for each cohort of artists from one annual event to the next. "Apply for the next cycle." "The Class of 2027."

**"Cohort"** is the internal operational term. Used in team communications, this document, and platform back-end systems. Never used in marketing, landing pages, or public communications.

## Operational Reality — What a Small Team Can Actually Do

A team of one to three people managing five artists through a full cohort year while simultaneously building and operating the platform is at the edge of what's possible. This section is an honest assessment of what gets done well, what gets stretched, and what the first cohort should actually look like.

### What the Platform Automates — Zero Team Labor Once Built
These cost time to build but near zero to operate per artist:
- All royalty registrations at onboarding
- Revenue ingestion and distribution to individuals
- 1099 generation at year end
- Social post drafting and approval workflow
- Streaming data pulling and dashboard updates
- Badge and unlock triggers
- Pledge raise and investment raise mechanics
- Automated marketing triggers along the journey

This is the leverage in the model. Build it once, it serves every artist forever.

### What Requires Human Attention Per Artist

| Task | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Artist onboarding and relationship | High — ongoing | The most important work |
| LLC formation coordination | Medium — one time | Per artist at signing |
| Recording coordination | Medium | Studio booking, scheduling |
| Mixing / mastering oversight | Low | Vendor managed |
| Music video coordination | Medium | Per artist |
| Sponsor outreach | High | Per artist, ongoing |
| Press and playlist pitching | High | Per artist, ongoing |
| Sync pitching | Medium | Ongoing |
| Live show booking support | Medium | Per artist |
| Investor communications | Medium | Ongoing |
| Event production | Very high | Once per year |
| Platform operations | Full time | One dedicated person minimum |

### The Three-Person Team Reality

**Person 1 — Operations / Artist Relations**
Manages all artist relationships day to day. Coordinates recording, mixing, mastering, video. Handles onboarding, LLC formation, studio coordination. Manageable at three artists. Stretched at five without systems fully operational.

**Person 2 — Marketing / Investor Relations**
Sponsor outreach, press, playlist pitching, sync pitching, investor communications, governance moments. A full-time role for five artists simultaneously. Realistically covers two or three artists deeply and gives lighter support to the others.

**Person 3 — Technical / Platform**
Keeps the platform running. Integrations connected. Dashboards accurate. Payment flows working. Builds the automation layer. Has zero bandwidth for artist management.

### What Realistically Gets Done for Five Artists in Year One

**Confidently achievable:**
- All artists fully onboarded with all registrations complete
- All LLCs formed, band agreements signed
- All investment raises completed
- All singles recorded, mixed, and mastered
- All singles released to all streaming platforms
- Basic merch run for each artist
- Automated social marketing running
- Earnings flowing and distributed correctly
- The annual event produced successfully

**Possible but stretched:**
- Music videos for all five — more likely two or three get proper videos, others get lyric videos or visualizers
- Sponsor partnerships for all five — more likely one or two land meaningful sponsors
- Active sync pitching for all five — more likely submitted to marketplace, actively pitched only for strongest candidates
- Full live show booking support for all five

**Realistically deferred to year two:**
- Deep individual press campaigns
- Regional show booking
- International rights registration
- Full video production for every artist

### Event Capacity as the Natural Scale Governor

The number of performers per event is capped at five — set by the stage, not by a business decision. But the number of artists signed in a given cycle is not the same as the number who perform. All signed artists attend Event One. Only the top five (ranked by pledge support) take the stage. The signing class can grow as operations allow without changing the event experience.

This separates two things that are easy to conflate: **the signing class** (can scale) and **the performance slots** (always five). Investors get more deal flow as the platform grows. The event never gets diluted. Both things are true at once.

Scale comes from growing the signing class and, eventually, adding more events per year in other cities — not from putting fifteen artists on one stage. A second annual event in a second city doubles the capacity without changing what either night feels like.

This is not a workaround for logistics — it is a philosophical choice. The scarcity of a performance slot is part of what makes performing matter. The signing class can expand; the stage stays intimate.

### The Honest Recommendation — Start With Three
The first cycle sets the standard everything is judged against. Three artists with an extraordinary, fully supported experience and documented results is a stronger foundation than five artists with a diluted one.

**Cycle 1 — Three artists. Prove the model. Build the playbook.**
**Cycle 2 — Five artists. Scale with confidence.**
**Cycle 3+ — Expand signing class as team, systems, and event capacity allow. Add a second event before overfilling the first.**

The event still works beautifully with three artists. Three IPOs on one night, one gong ring, one extraordinary evening. The intimacy of three might actually be more powerful than five for the inaugural event.

## Founding Team & Partnership Needs

The vision behind SoundSteak is clear, deep, and genuinely original. The hardest and rarest role in any venture is the visionary — the person who understands what the thing needs to be before it exists. That role is filled. What makes the rest possible is surrounding it with the right people.

### The Founder's Role — Honest Assessment

**What the founder brings:**
- The original vision and its depth — years of thinking about what artists actually need
- Decades of experience as a working musician in local and regional bands — not major label experience, but the authentic perspective of the artist the platform is actually built for
- The product compass — the instinct for what is right and what isn't, what serves artists and what exploits them
- The existing network — bands, studio owners, show organizers, warm industry contacts
- The technical build — the platform is being built via AI-assisted development (Claude Code), which is what makes the build feasible at all. The founder is the builder.
- Event and sponsorship experience — organized a yearly event, built sponsorship packages, designed sponsor guides

**What the founder is not:**
- A major label industry insider — the experience is local and regional, which is honest and actually appropriate for a platform targeting emerging artists
- The public face of the company — this role needs someone else
- A trained securities attorney or financial professional

**Why local musician experience is the right credibility:**
The platform is designed for local and emerging artists — people exactly like the founder. Understanding that world from the inside, knowing the frustrations of playing bars and small venues, watching money disappear, not knowing what an IPI number is — that is the precise lived experience this platform was built to solve. It is more credible to the target user than any major label background could be. The founder is not building for artists they've observed. They are building for artists they are.

**The founder's role: Chief Product Officer & Builder**
Holds the vision. Builds the platform. Guards the artist-first philosophy against every pressure to compromise it. Makes product decisions. Activates the existing network. The engine of the build.

**The origin story:**
SoundSteak was born from a misunderstanding. The founder encountered the concept of fan ownership of music through NFT-based platforms — and didn't fully understand that NFTs were the vehicle, not the idea. What registered was the concept itself: fans owning a piece of the music they believed in. That idea was worth building. The NFT part, it turned out, was exactly what killed every platform that used it — an unregulated, speculative vehicle that collapsed under its own weight and SEC scrutiny.

The misunderstanding produced the correct answer. By not fixating on the crypto mechanism, the founder built toward what fan ownership of music actually needed to be: a legally sound securities structure, an LLC per release, a real event, a real contract, a real label. The thing every NFT platform was trying to approximate but couldn't — because they started with the technology instead of the idea.

Royal.io raised $71M and is gone. Sound.xyz raised $20M and pivoted. The concept survived. The vehicle didn't. SoundSteak is what happens when someone builds the concept without the baggage.

## Pledge Integrity & Anti-Fraud

The pledge system is the foundation of the platform's legitimacy. 300 real fans backing an artist is what gives the label mandate and the gong meaning. One person running 300 fake profiles doesn't prove anything — it corrupts everything. The system has to make that structurally impossible, not just against the rules.

This section covers the threat model and the layered defenses.

---

### The Threat Model

Bad actors will attempt to manipulate the pledge system in the following ways:

**Multiple accounts, one person:**
The most obvious attack. One person creates dozens or hundreds of profiles to back their own artist or kill a competitor's chances. Motivations: vanity (I got signed), sabotage (they didn't), financial (manufactured investor demand).

**Payment farming:**
Using the same payment method across multiple accounts — one debit card, one credit card, one bank account — to fund multiple pledges from a single source of funds.

**Prepaid and gift card evasion:**
Once the same-card block is in place, a determined bad actor buys 300 Visa gift cards. Each card is technically distinct. This is expensive but not impossible, especially for an artist or their team trying to guarantee a signing.

**Device and network clustering:**
All 300 fake profiles created and funded from the same device, the same IP address, the same home network, or the same geographic location in a short time window.

**Synthetic identities:**
Fake names, fake email addresses, throwaway phone numbers. Each profile looks superficially legitimate but has no real person behind it.

**Coordinated bot campaigns:**
Automated scripts creating profiles and pledging at scale — faster than any human team could manage manually.

**Colluding real people:**
An artist's family and friends all pledge but funnel money from a single source — Venmo'd or cash-handed from the artist to 30 real people who each make a real profile and pledge with their own card. Technically real, structurally manufactured.

---

### Layer 1 — Payment Fingerprinting

**Same card, same account:**
One payment method can only be linked to one SoundSteak profile, ever. A card number — hashed and stored, never raw — is flagged the moment it appears on a second profile. The second profile receives a clear error message: *"This payment method is already associated with another account."* No exception.

**Prepaid and gift card rejection:**
Visa, Mastercard, and Amex prepaid BINs (Bank Identification Numbers — the first 6 digits of any card) are publicly known and catalogued. Prepaid cards are blocked at the point of entry — no exceptions. The platform requires a payment method with a traceable financial identity behind it: a debit card linked to a real bank account or a credit card issued to a verified person. No dark money. No anonymous purchasing power. The pledge system is a provenance record, and every dollar in it must have a clear, accountable origin.

**Bank account linking (ACH):**
Where bank accounts are used instead of cards, the account + routing number combination is treated as a unique identity. One bank account, one profile.

**Velocity checks:**
If 20+ pledges to the same artist arrive from distinct payment methods within a narrow time window, that cohort is flagged for review. Organic fans don't all pledge in the same 4-minute window. Coordinated campaigns do.

---

### Layer 2 — Identity Signals

**One phone number, one profile:**
Phone verification is required at sign-up. A phone number can only be associated with one account. SMS verification at pledge time adds a second confirmation that the number is active and in the person's possession. VoIP numbers and number-recycling services are detected and rejected — these are the tool of choice for mass fake-account creation.

**One email address, one profile:**
Trivial to enforce. Plus-addressing (user+1@gmail.com, user+2@gmail.com) is normalized to its root address.

**Government ID for investment-level stakes:**
At the $1 pledge level, the bar is intentionally low — we are trying to be accessible. But once a pledge converts to a Founding SteakHolder stake and real money is invested, KYC (Know Your Customer) identity verification is triggered for any user above a defined threshold. ID verification is standard in financial products and the platform will integrate with a provider (Persona, Jumio, Stripe Identity, or equivalent) to automate it. This catches synthetic identities that passed the low-barrier pledge stage.

---

### Layer 3 — Behavioral & Device Signals

**Device fingerprinting:**
Browser fingerprints — GPU, screen resolution, font list, language settings, installed plugins — are highly unique. A device that creates multiple accounts, even with different emails and phone numbers, generates a matching fingerprint. Multiple accounts from the same fingerprint are flagged.

**IP clustering:**
Multiple accounts created or pledges submitted from the same IP address in a short window are flagged. Residential proxies and VPNs are detected using commercial databases (IPQualityScore or equivalent). A pledge from a known data center IP is treated as bot-risk.

**Account age vs. pledge timing:**
An account created 4 minutes before pledging for an artist, with no prior platform activity, is a much higher-risk signal than an account that has existed for 6 weeks and has browsed other artist profiles. New accounts can pledge — early fans are real — but a mass of brand-new accounts all pledging the same artist in the same session is a pattern, not a coincidence.

**Geographic clustering:**
300 pledges from the same ZIP code to a band based in a different city is anomalous. The platform tracks pledge geography as part of artist audience data anyway — anomalous clustering is surfaced in the review queue automatically.

**Behavioral velocity:**
A normal user takes 30–120 seconds to read a profile, navigate to the pledge button, and complete the flow. Pledges completing in under 10 seconds are bot-pattern.

---

### Layer 4 — Manual Review Triggers

Some clusters of signals don't warrant automatic rejection but do warrant a human looking at the data before the pledge counts. Triggers include:

- More than 5 pledges from the same device fingerprint
- More than 10 pledges from the same IP or subnet
- A single artist receiving 50%+ of their pledges within a 2-hour window
- Any artist whose pledge count goes from 0 to 200+ in under 24 hours
- Geographic distribution significantly narrower than the artist's stated market

Manual review does not freeze the pledge campaign. Flagged pledges are held in a provisional state — they count toward the visible total but are marked for review. If review confirms fraud, they are removed and the count adjusts. If review confirms legitimacy, they clear automatically. Provisional counts are not surfaced publicly — the visible number is always the confirmed number.

---

### Layer 5 — The Colluding Real People Problem

This is the hardest case. Thirty real people, real phones, real cards, real identities — all paid by the artist to pledge. Technically, the system can't distinguish this from 30 genuine fans.

The response is structural, not technical:

**The pledge is public record.** Every Founding SteakHolder's pledge date is documented on their profile, permanently. A SteakHolder with a 3-day-old account and no prior platform history who pledged the same night as 29 other 3-day-old accounts sends a signal. The community will notice. It is documented. It becomes part of the artist's provenance — and provenance is the asset.

**The gong is the real test.** A manufactured pledge campaign may win the signing. But the gong rings, the LLC forms, and actual investors commit real money in the open raise that follows. If the 300 "fans" were manufactured, there are no real fans behind them. That will surface immediately in the open raise, in stream performance, in merch pre-order numbers, in every downstream metric. A manufactured signing is its own punishment — the artist is now publicly accountable to a community that doesn't exist.

**Consequences are permanent.** A profile found to have participated in fraudulent pledging is removed from the platform, permanently.
