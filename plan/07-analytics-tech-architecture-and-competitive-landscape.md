## The Year in Review — But With Data Nobody Else Has

Spotify Wrapped works because it tells people something about themselves they didn't know — and gives them something shareable that feels personal. SoundSteak's version does the same, but with a layer of data Spotify will never have.

Spotify knows you listened to an artist 847 times. SoundSteak knows you were one of the first 50 people to pledge on them, that your shares brought in 12 new SteakHolders, that your stake in their single earned you $34.17 this year, and that you voted for the producer who ended up making the record.

That is not a listening recap. That is a story about the role you played in something real.

### What the Annual SoundSteak Wrap Could Include

**For fans and investors:**
- Your portfolio performance — total earnings across all stakes this year, broken down by artist and product
- Your earliest bets — artists you backed before they broke, ranked by how early you were
- Your influence score — how many people joined the platform through content you shared
- Your governance footprint — votes you participated in and how those outcomes shaped the platform
- Your cut this year — the total dollar amount your stakes generated, visualized as it grew month by month
- The ripple report — the downstream chain of influence from your earliest share of each artist

**For artists:**
- The full year of growth visualized — streaming velocity, audience expansion, geographic spread, month by month
- Who found you first — the earliest SteakHolders, the first cities, the first countries
- The journey recap — every milestone hit, every unlock triggered, every investment round that closed
- The people who made it happen — producer, engineer, studio, photographer, every credited contributor
- What your community did — Cues responded to, collab choices made, merch designs selected

**For professionals:**
- Every project contributed to this year, ranked by performance
- Verified credits accumulated — a living resume built by the platform automatically
- The value your work generated for artists and investors — not self-reported, system-verified

### The Design Philosophy for Year in Review
The Spotify Wrapped model succeeds on shareability. Every card is designed to be posted. SoundSteak's version must do the same — but the data is inherently more interesting because it includes financial outcomes, influence chains, and community roles that no streaming platform can touch.

The challenge is making financial data feel emotional rather than transactional. "You earned $34.17" is less compelling than "The artist you believed in before anyone else paid you back this year." Same fact. Different story. The presentation must always lead with the human meaning of the number, not the number itself.

### The Standing Mandate
From the moment data begins flowing through the system, everyone building and operating this platform watches for novel ways to capture and present it. The right ideas will not all be visible from here — they emerge from seeing real people interact with real data in ways nobody predicted.

The question to ask constantly: *what does this platform know that no other platform knows — and what does that mean to the person it's about?*

Every answer to that question is a feature waiting to be built.

## The Community Vote — Fan-Driven Collab Invitations

Every two weeks, the platform surfaces a new vote: **Who would you most want to see collab on a single?**

The community nominates and votes. The result is not a poll result — it is an invitation. SoundSteak publishes the outcome publicly and sends a formal invitation to the winning artist(s), signed by the platform and backed by the visible size and enthusiasm of the vote.

### Why This Works

The vote result is the pitch. An artist receiving an invitation already knows: a real audience actively wants this, campaigned for it, and is watching to see what happens. That is not a cold outreach. That is leverage.

The charity angle makes the invitation structurally difficult to decline. If the community has surfaced a cause the invited artist already publicly supports — through their own social platforms, their prior interviews, their known affiliations — the invitation becomes: "Your fans and [collab artist]'s fans are asking you to do something for a cause you already believe in." Declining that publicly is an awkward thing to do. Saying yes signals something real about the artist's character.

### How the Charity Works

There is no single designated charity per vote. The community can associate the invitation with any cause the artists involved are known to care about — and multiple charities can be named. An artist who cares about mental health access, and another who has spoken about environmental issues, can each have their cause acknowledged in the same invitation. The platform supports multiple charity designations per collab single. The percentage directed to each is agreed upon at signing and flows automatically through the distribution system, exactly like any other ownership split.

### The Full Participation Rule

This is foundational and non-negotiable: **every artist participating in a collab vote project participates in the platform fully.** There is no guest track path. No honorary credit. No lend-your-name-and-leave arrangement.

An established artist who says yes to a collab invitation joins the cycle. They go through the LLC formation, the full contract, the full investment structure, the milestone journey, the year of transparency. Their earnings are public. Their splits are documented. They are a SoundSteak artist for that project.

This makes the invitation more meaningful, not less. The artist who accepts is making a real commitment — and they know it before they say yes. The invitation letter is explicit about what participation involves. An artist who says yes to that is making a statement about what they believe in, not just doing a charity cameo.

It also means the collab single carries the same investor structure, the same SteakHolder access, the same LLC ownership as every other release on the platform. An established artist's name on a properly structured release, with transparent earnings and an investor pool, is an enormous signal to the market.

### The Cadence

One vote every two weeks. Enough frequency to feel alive — the community always has an active vote, a result arriving soon, and an invitation being watched. Not so frequent that each vote loses its moment or the community's attention fragments.

Each vote has a two-week window for community nominations and voting, followed by a public result announcement and the formal invitation. The invitation has a defined response window. If the artist declines or doesn't respond, the result is published transparently and the next vote opens. No outcome is hidden — the community that voted deserves to know what their vote produced.

### The Result Is Always Content

Whether the artist says yes or no, the vote result is a story:
- "15,000 people voted for this. Here's the letter we sent." — Published.
- An artist accepts — an extraordinary story about what the community made happen.
- An artist declines gracefully — published, and a signal that at least they engaged.
- No response — published, with the community's vote on record.

The transparency of the attempt is itself valuable. An artist who watches a vote they weren't part of, sees the community enthusiasm, and thinks "I'd want to be in the next one" — that is an acquisition mechanism no ad budget produces.

### Open Questions — Community Vote

- [ ] Who can nominate artists — any platform member, or verified SteakHolders only?
- [ ] Is there a minimum vote count before an invitation is sent, or does every vote result generate one?
- [ ] What is the invitation response window — two weeks? Thirty days?
- [ ] Does the platform attempt direct contact with the artist's management, or is the invitation public-only until they initiate?
- [ ] Is the charity surfaced by the community during the vote, or proposed by the platform based on known artist affiliations?
- [ ] What happens if both nominated artists say yes but one is already mid-cycle — do they collab on the next available cycle, or does the platform accommodate them immediately?

## Data & Analytics Philosophy

The platform takes a Webull approach to data density — not Robinhood's minimalism. Robinhood reduced friction for casual investors by hiding depth. SoundSteak gives investors everything they want to chew on, updated in real time. For an unfamiliar asset class like music, more information builds more confidence.

### Design Philosophy
Webull-dense but music-native. The visual language feels like a music platform with exceptional financial depth underneath — not a trading terminal with album art. Data is available for the investor who wants to go deep, but the surface layer still feels like discovering artists, not reading earnings reports.

### Artist-Level Analytics
- Streaming numbers across all platforms, updated live
- Stream velocity — momentum trending up or down week over week
- Geographic heat map — where in the world the artist is being heard
- Playlist placements — how many, what size, what genre
- Social following growth across all platforms
- Engagement rate — active audience, not just follower count

### Investment Analytics
- Current stake value (if secondary market exists)
- Earnings to date — total and broken down by source
- Earnings per stream equivalent
- Projected annual yield based on current trajectory
- Comparison benchmarks — how this artist performs vs. others at the same milestone stage
- Historical earnings chart — scrubbable graph in the Webull style

### Product-Level Analytics
- Per-song and per-album performance tracked separately
- Revenue breakdown by source — streaming, sync, live, merch, YouTube
- What percentage of total earnings each product contributes
- Sync placement tracker — when a song lands in a film, TV show, or ad, it surfaces visibly

### Showcase & Market Analytics
- How many investors are watching a particular artist
- Pledge raise progress in real time — live bar filling on showcase night
- Investment round fill rate — how quickly a raise is closing
- Comparable artist benchmarks at the same stage

### Why This Feeds the Flywheel
Rich public-facing artist data gives fans and casual investors a reason to return and engage even when they're not actively investing. Watching your artist grow is part of the experience. The data layer turns passive stakeholders into active followers.

## Technical Architecture

### The Honest Reality — What This System Actually Is

SoundSteak is not one product. It is the unified interface over the top of what are currently multiple separate industries, each served by companies that do only one thing.

| What SoundSteak Does | Companies That Do Only This |
|---|---|
| Music distribution | DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby |
| Streaming royalty collection | Spotify for Artists, Apple Music for Artists |
| Performance royalty administration | ASCAP, BMI, SESAC |
| Mechanical royalty collection | MLC, Songtrust |
| Digital performance royalties | SoundExchange |
| Publishing administration | Songtrust, Downtown Music, CD Baby Pro |
| Securities offering & cap table management | Carta, AngelList, Republic |
| Investment platform & portfolio dashboard | Robinhood, Webull, Republic |
| Payment processing & multi-party distribution | Stripe, Dwolla, PayPal |
| Ticketing | Eventbrite, DICE, Bandsintown |
| Merch fulfillment & e-commerce | Shopify, Printful, Printify |
| Point of sale | Square, Toast, Stripe Terminal |
| Contracts & e-signature | DocuSign, HelloSign |
| Governance & voting | Custom — no existing solution fits |
| Social & automated marketing | Hootsuite, Buffer, Later |
| Music player | Spotify, Apple Music, SoundCloud |
| Artist analytics dashboard | Chartmetric, Soundcharts |
| Tax & 1099 generation | Gusto, QuickBooks, ADP |
| Sync licensing marketplace | Musicbed, Artlist, Musicbed |

Every one of those companies employs full engineering teams focused exclusively on their one function. SoundSteak needs all of them working together, talking to each other, feeding a single unified dashboard, and distributing through a single payment infrastructure.

That is a genuinely enormous undertaking. There is no point pretending otherwise.

### The Strategy — Integration Layer First, Proprietary Infrastructure Over Time

The platform cannot be built from scratch all at once. Attempting to do so would take years and tens of millions of dollars before a single artist is signed. That is not the path.

**The day one architecture is an integration layer** — a unified interface that connects existing best-in-class services via their APIs and presents everything through a single dashboard. SoundSteak does not replace DistroKid on day one. It connects to DistroKid and pulls its data into the platform. It does not replace Stripe on day one — it uses Stripe Connect as the payment backbone. It does not replace Eventbrite — it integrates with Eventbrite's API for ticketing data.

The platform's proprietary value on day one is:
- The unified dashboard no other service provides
- The cap table and ownership management layer connecting everything
- The automatic distribution logic that splits every incoming payment correctly
- The governance and voting system — no existing solution does what this needs
- The artist journey management and automated marketing layer
- The conversational voice interface that no existing platform has

Everything else is an API call to an existing service.

**Proprietary infrastructure is built progressively** as volume justifies replacing a third-party service with an in-house equivalent. This mirrors the vertical integration roadmap — preferred partner first, equity stake later, ownership eventually. The same logic applies to software: use the existing service first, build the replacement when the platform is large enough to justify it.

### The MVP Infrastructure Stack — What Must Work on Night One

The first annual event requires exactly this to function:

**Payments & Distribution**
- Stripe Connect — multi-party payment processing, automatic split distribution, 1099 generation
- Bank account verification for every participant profile before any payment moves

**Artist & Investor Onboarding**
- DocuSign or equivalent — digital contracts signed before the event
- LLC formation integration (Clerky, Stripe Atlas, or Doola) — triggered at signing
- PRO registration flow — guided, platform-assisted, completed within 48 hours of signing

**The Pledge Raise**
- Custom-built raise mechanics on top of Stripe Connect
- Real-time pledge counter visible publicly during the event
- Immediate equity documentation upon pledge completion

**Music Distribution**
- DistroKid or TuneCore partnership — platform submits releases on artist's behalf
- API connection established so earnings data pulls automatically when available

**The Dashboard**
- Artist profile with connected streaming data (Spotify for Artists API, Apple Music for Artists API)
- Investor view showing stakes owned, earnings to date, pipeline
- Public view of every artist's earnings — the transparency layer
- Real-time transaction feed — every dollar that moves through the system appears immediately

**POS App**
- Built on Stripe Terminal — card reader + custom app for cash logging
- Artist installs before first show, all merch and door transactions run through it

**The Music Player**
- Minimum viable player on day one — playback, queue, share functionality
- The investment and story layer built in from the start, even if simple initially

**Contracts**
- Band agreement — platform standard template, e-signed by all members at onboarding
- Single recording contract — one template, used for every artist, every cohort
- Investor disclosure — Reg A+ compliant, reviewed by securities attorney before use

## Competitive Landscape

Full competitive research is in `/Users/donovanbrigham/Desktop/SoundSteak Research/competitive-intel/` with individual files on each platform and regulatory path.

### Direct Competitors — Current Status

- **Royal.io** — Shut down 2024. Raised $71M. Fan royalty tokens for catalog songs (Nas, The Chainsmokers). NFT-based structure crumbled under SEC pressure and crypto market collapse. Right concept, wrong legal vehicle.
- **SongVest** — Active. Reg A+ securities. $20 minimum investment. 17–49% sourcing fee (!) charged to artists. Focuses on existing catalog, not new releases. Legal path is correct but the fee structure is extractive.
- **Royalty Exchange** — Buy/sell existing music earnings catalogs. Backward-looking, no new artist infrastructure.
- **ANote Music** — European music investment platform.
- **Anotherblock** — Stockholm-based, NFT/Ethereum. No US regulatory path. Real pricing data: Rihanna's "Bitch Better Have My Money" sold 300 slots at $210 each for 0.0033% royalty share each.
- **JKBX (Jukebox)** — Reg A+ filer, detailed public SEC filings available. Good structural blueprint.
- **Sound.xyz** — Raised $20M. Music NFT marketplace, 100% primary sales to artists. Pivoted to subscription model (Sound Premium) in 2024 when NFT market collapsed.

### The Pattern No Competitor Escaped

Every NFT-based model failed. Royal.io (dead), Anotherblock (EU-only), Sound.xyz (pivoted). The concept of fan ownership and fractional investment in music is correct. The crypto/NFT vehicle was the problem — it bypassed US securities law, excluded mainstream investors, and collapsed when token markets collapsed.

SongVest proved the LLC + Reg A+ path is the legally durable structure. Their flaw is fee extraction and backward-looking focus on existing catalog.

### Key Differentiators

- **Forward-looking** — investing before the record exists, not in proven catalog
- **Full label infrastructure** — not just an investment marketplace
- **LLC per release** — formed at contract signing, investors own an actual legal entity
- **Live event as IPO mechanism** — the gong rings, ownership opens for sale
- **Real-time earnings tracking** — vs. quarterly/semi-annual at competitors
- **First come first served** — no minimums, no maximums; pure speed-of-access
- **Governance and voting rights** for shareholders
- **Individual-level payment** — money never pools in a band account
- **Comprehensive onboarding** — all registrations and metadata handled automatically
- **Defined exit (buyout) terms** written into every contract from day one
- **Milestone progression model**
- **Flywheel growth** — platform grows itself through nominations and invites
- **Exclusivity** — music player plays only SoundSteak-contracted, investor-backed releases
- **AI-ready catalog with contextual data** — artist intent, influences, mood, instrumentation, lyrical themes captured at intake and throughout the release year; no scraped catalog can replicate it
- **Developer console as B2B product** — metered licensed access to catalog + context for AI companies, sync supervisors, app developers, and AI agents; a revenue stream no competing label offers
- **AI company partnership potential** — clean provenance + rich context + consent-documented licensing makes SoundSteak the only viable partner for frontier AI labs that need music data they can legally and ethically use

## The Annual Event — A Three-Act Show

The annual event is not a showcase. It is not a competition. It is not an awards show. It is all three — a single evening that functions as the cultural heartbeat of the entire platform.

### Act One — The Awards
The cohort that has been active all year gets recognized. Every award is backed by data the entire community has watched accumulate in real time. No industry insiders deciding outcomes. No popularity contests. No politics. A band from a small town with no connections wins because the numbers say they win. That is a radical statement in an industry where awards have always been about who you know.

**What makes these awards different:**
The Grammys are voted on by industry insiders. The AMAs are popularity contests. The VMAs are televised marketing. SoundSteak awards are grounded in transparent, publicly tracked data that has been visible to every stakeholder all year. Nobody can argue with the results because everyone watched them happen.

**Award categories — Artist Performance:**
- Highest streaming growth — most velocity over the year
- Fastest funded raise — investment round that filled quickest
- Largest audience reached — geographic spread, new markets entered
- Most playlist placements
- Most sync placements — songs placed in film, TV, ads
- Runaway of the Year — the artist who most exceeded expectations across all metrics

**Award categories — Investor Performance:**
- Best performing single — highest return to investors
- Most backed artist — most individual investors in a single raise
- Earliest believer — investor who pledged first on the year's breakthrough artist

**Award categories — Community:**
- Most active governance participant — investor who voted most throughout the year
- Most collab nominations that led to a completed project
- Most engaged cohort — class with highest overall community activity

**Award categories — The Event Itself:**
- Audience favorite — live vote on showcase night
- Critics choice — panel of working musicians
- Investor pick — existing platform investors vote ahead of the event

### Act Two — The Graduation (Event Two for last cycle's artists)
Last cycle's artists perform their finished singles. The payoff moment. The room hears what a year of investment, production, and community support sounds like on a stage. Investors who funded these recordings hear them performed live for the first time. The artists who received their contracts at last year's event take the stage as released artists. This is Event Two of their journey — the single release, one year after Event One.

### Act Three — The Signing (Event One for the new cycle)
New artists perform. The fans who believe in them have already pledged — the room knows who they are. Selected artists receive their contracts on stage. When the gong rings, ownership of each new single's LLC goes live for purchase simultaneously with the contract award. New investors back the next class. The cycle begins again.

The same evening is Event Two for artists graduating and Event One for artists signing. This is the annual event's structural elegance: every year, one class closes and a new one opens in the same room on the same night.

### The Production Reality
This is a full evening production. Red carpet. Performances. Awards. New signings. It needs:
- A host
- Full production value — lighting, sound, staging
- Filming for content distribution
- Live streaming for people who cannot be in the room
- Press access

**The sponsorship value multiplied:** A title sponsor is not putting their name on a local showcase. They are putting their name on an awards show with transparent, data-backed outcomes and a highly engaged, investing audience. That is a completely different conversation with a completely different budget.

### The Name
Whatever this event is called needs to carry the weight of all three acts. A showcase name is not big enough. This needs to feel like the Grammys meets Tiny Desk meets a startup demo day — but entirely its own thing with its own name that eventually stands alone as a cultural brand.

**This is one of the most important open creative decisions in the entire platform.**
