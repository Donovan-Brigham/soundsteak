## The Single as the Complete Business Model

Every artist journey on SoundSteak is a journey to one thing: a single release that earns as much as possible across every available income channel. Not a series of managed investment rounds. One focused mission with as many income-generating variables attached to it as the platform can activate.

This is what a major label team does for a handful of priority artists. SoundSteak automates that infrastructure and delivers it to every signed artist — because the system does the work the team would otherwise do.

### Every Income Variable Attached to One Single

| Income Stream | How the Platform Activates It |
|---|---|
| Streaming | Distribution handled, metadata perfect, all platforms covered from day one |
| Live shows | Booking support, platform POS at every show, earnings tracked automatically |
| Merch | Platform fulfillment tied to the release cycle, earnings flow through system |
| Sync licensing | Song listed in sync marketplace, actively pitched to supervisors |
| Sponsors | Brand partnerships matched to artist's audience data, platform pursues on artist's behalf |
| YouTube / Content ID | Every video monetized automatically via auth connection |
| Beat marketplace | If producer is on platform, their stake is live and earnings route automatically |
| Fan tipping | Direct fan support through the platform at shows and online |
| Playlist pitching | Platform submits to playlist curators using connected streaming data |

### The Unlock Model — Badges Open Doors
The journey is a progression of unlocks. Each achievement opens the next opportunity. The platform doesn't hand everything to an artist on day one — they earn access to each layer by performing at the current one.

| Achievement | What It Unlocks |
|---|---|
| Pledge raise funded | LLC formation, onboarding infrastructure begins |
| Investment raise fully funded | Recording budget released, studio booking opens |
| Recording complete | Mixing and mastering funded, music video planning begins |
| Single released | Earnings tracking begins across all streams |
| 1K streams | First merch raise unlocks |
| First merch run sold out | Second merch raise, live show booking support opens |
| First live show completed | Tour planning support unlocks |
| 10K streams | Sync licensing marketplace listing activates |
| Fully Funded badge | Sponsor matching unlocks — platform actively pursues brand partners |
| Sold Out badge | Booking agent connections unlock |
| 100K streams | Second single contract available — journey begins again |

### The Investor Perspective
Investors didn't back an investment round. They backed a single. Every income stream the platform unlocks — the merch, the shows, the sync placements, the sponsors — is additional return on the same investment that they didn't have to predict or price in. Every new channel the platform activates is upside that compounds. That's a more compelling proposition than any single-purpose investment vehicle.

### The Sponsor Unlock
An artist with a Fully Funded badge, growing streaming numbers, and a sold-out show is a provably attractive partner for a brand. The platform approaches sponsors on behalf of artists with real-time, transparent, verifiable data — not a pitch deck with inflated claims. A brand can look at the same dashboard any investor sees. That's a sponsor conversation most emerging artists could never have on their own and that no traditional label makes available at this career stage.

## Artist Journey Management & Automated Marketing

The platform is not passive infrastructure. It actively works for the artist at every stage of their journey — knowing what they have before they declare it, knowing what moment they're in, and taking intentional marketing action automatically.

### The First Moment
The instant auth connections are established, the platform builds a complete artist profile before a single form is filled out:
- Follower counts across every platform
- Strongest audience platform and demographic
- Geographic listener distribution
- Existing posting history and cadence
- Current streaming trajectory and velocity
- Existing releases, merch stores, and upcoming shows
- Engagement rate vs. follower count — the true measure of audience loyalty

The artist opens SoundSteak for the first time and their dashboard is already populated. That first moment is a product statement: *we already know you, we're already working for you.*

### Write Access to Social — The Right Approach
The platform drafts the post. The artist approves with one tap. It goes.

Not fully autonomous posting — that removes the artist's voice and creates risk. But removing the friction of knowing what to say, opening each app, and remembering to post is where most artists fall down. The platform does the thinking. The artist does the approving.

Over time the system learns the artist's voice from their existing post history and drafts content that sounds like them — not a press release.

### The Journey
Every artist moves through a defined journey. The platform knows exactly where each artist is and fires the right marketing action at the right moment — intentional, automated, and timed to maximize impact.

| Journey Stage | Trigger | Platform Action |

### What This Means for Investors
Every automated marketing action the platform takes on the artist's behalf directly serves investors. A well-marketed artist earns more. More earnings means better dividends. The platform's marketing engine is an investment protection mechanism — not just an artist convenience.

### The Fan Intelligence Layer

Independent artists have always operated blind. They have follower counts and a bar chart on Spotify for Artists. That is the entirety of fan intelligence available to them today. Major labels have entire departments doing what this platform builds in by default — and they have never shared any of it with the artists who generated the audience.

SoundSteak changes that completely. Every fan interaction that touches the platform is a documented data point. Over time, those data points compose a real, navigable picture of each artist's audience — not a number, a population.

**What the platform knows about an artist's fans:**

- **Who found them first** — the documented early believers: date of first pledge, how they discovered the artist, what they did next
- **Who drives others in** — the attribution chain. Which fans share and generate new pledges, listeners, or investors. The top ten people responsible for audience growth, ranked and visible
- **Where the audience actually lives** — geographic breakdown by city and region, compared against where the artist has played and where they haven't. The gap between where fans are and where the artist tours is an opportunity the platform surfaces directly
- **How engagement differs by platform** — a fan who follows on Instagram behaves differently from one who follows on TikTok. A listener who found them on Spotify behaves differently from one who came through a share link. The platform tracks these populations separately and surfaces what works on each
- **What content makes fans act** — not just what performs well, but what drives pledges, shares, pre-orders, and investment. The platform correlates content type and timing with downstream commercial behavior, not just engagement rate
- **Who the SteakHolders are as people** — not demographics pulled from a third party, but self-declared data from platform profiles. What other artists they back. What roles they hold. How active they are in governance. Their engagement history with this specific release
- **Conversion patterns** — how long it takes a new fan to become a pledger, a pledger to become an investor, a listener to become a pre-order. Where in that funnel people fall off, and what content or outreach historically accelerates the conversion

**How it surfaces to the artist:**

Not as a data dump. As a weekly intelligence digest — synthesized by the platform's AI into plain language with specific recommendations:

> *"Your three strongest markets right now are Nashville, Toronto, and Atlanta. You've played Nashville twice. You've never played Toronto or Atlanta. Your engagement rate in both cities is higher than Nashville — which suggests audiences there are hungry and undersupplied. A single show in either market before the release would likely convert a significant number of fans to pre-orders."*

> *"The fans who came through Morgan's share link in March have a 4x higher conversion rate to investment than fans who found you through the playlist. Morgan is your highest-value attribution source. There's a governance thank-you mechanism available if you want to use it."*

> *"You have 47 SteakHolders. Twelve of them have profiles showing they're also invested in three or more other artists this cycle. That group has the highest governance participation rate on the platform — they are your most engaged investors and the most likely to share your release in their own networks."*

The artist who has this data makes better decisions about where to tour, what content to make, who to thank, and what to prioritize next. The SteakHolder who knows the platform is generating this intelligence for their artist has confidence the investment is being actively managed — not just passively tracked.

### Conversation as the Primary Interface

The milestone table above and the fan intelligence digest are both outputs of the same underlying approach. But the interface through which an artist experiences all of it is not a dashboard.

**It is a conversation.**

The platform engages every artist proactively, in natural language, as an ongoing relationship. Not notifications. Not task lists. Not badges unlocked. A conversation — the way a great manager would reach out, the way a trusted collaborator would check in.

> *"Hey Brian — you had studio time blocked for Saturday. How did the drum tracking go? Did you get through everything or did you end up pushing the overhead mics to next week?"*

That message serves three functions simultaneously. It's a check-in. It's data collection for the milestone system. It's a relationship. Brian answers in whatever way feels natural. The platform extracts what it needs. He never filled out a form.

When the platform needs something from Brian — a context task, a milestone update, a Cue, an intake question it didn't get at signing — it asks in conversation at the natural moment, not as a cold administrative prompt. The conversation is calibrated to where Brian is in his journey and what the system currently needs from him. It knows what happened last week. It knows what's coming next. It asks the question that's actually relevant right now.

**The hierarchy of engagement:**

1. **Conversation** — the primary interface. Proactive, warm, relational, contextual. Meets the artist in the moment they're in. On the platform, via text, via email — whichever channel they're in.
2. **Tasks** — surfaced in conversation first. If the conversation didn't extract what the system needs, the task appears on the dashboard as a structured fallback.
3. **Notifications** — for genuinely time-sensitive signals that can't wait for the next natural conversation moment.
4. **Gamification and badges** — celebrate what the conversation already produced. The milestone wasn't completed when a box was checked. It was completed when Brian said the drums were done. The badge marks the moment.

Legacy tech — notifications, tasks, gamification — doesn't go away. It becomes the safety net, not the experience. The cold task list is what an artist sees if they never engage with the conversation layer. The relationship is what they experience if they do.

**Multi-channel continuity:**

The conversation lives everywhere the artist is. On the platform dashboard. As a text message. In email. If an artist hasn't opened the app in ten days, the platform texts them. If they reply by text, that reply feeds back into the same conversation thread. The channel doesn't matter. The relationship is continuous.

This is not a chatbot. It is not a FAQ interface. It is a persistent, context-loaded, relationship-oriented intelligence that knows this specific artist, their specific situation, and what they need right now — and engages them about it in the way a person would.

### AI Trajectory Review — Regular Cadence

On a weekly and monthly cadence, the platform's AI agent conducts a structured review of each artist's journey against three reference points:

1. **Their stated goals** — set at intake, updated through conversation
2. **Their cohort calendar** — where they should be at this point in the year
3. **Platform-wide patterns** — what artists with similar profiles, genres, and audience shapes have historically done at this stage

The review produces one of three outputs:

- **On track** — brief confirmation surfaced in the weekly digest. No action needed.
- **Ahead of schedule** — specific unlock triggers surfaced early. Milestone recalibrated forward.
- **Behind** — specific diagnosis of what is lagging and what intervention is indicated. Surfaced first to the artist in conversation, then to SteakHolders in their digest if the deviation is significant.

The artist never receives a cold performance report. They receive a conversation:

> *"Looking at where you are against the cohort calendar — you're about two weeks behind where I'd expect on the recording completion milestone. Is that because the session got pushed, or did something come up with the producer situation you mentioned last month? Knowing which it is changes what I'd suggest doing next."*

The system doesn't just report deviation. It tries to understand it, because the cause determines the response. A delayed studio session is solved differently from a delayed funding threshold. The conversation extracts the context the data doesn't have.

**The SteakHolder early warning system:**

When trajectory deviation is significant — not a two-week slip but a pattern that historically precedes threshold failure — SteakHolders receive a proactive digest item flagging it. Not alarm. Context:

> *"The Hollow North is currently tracking behind the cohort calendar by approximately three weeks on the recording completion milestone. This is within the normal range of variation at this stage. We're in conversation with the artist about the cause and will update you if the trajectory changes materially."*

SteakHolders find out at month three, not month ten. That is the difference between a manageable situation and a crisis.

## Revenue Collection Infrastructure — The Full Picture

Every dollar an artist earns needs to flow through the platform. Every transaction needs to be tracked, attributed, and publicly displayed in real time. That is the vision. What follows is an honest assessment of what that looks like in practice — what is achievable from day one, what requires building, and what requires the platform to become something bigger than a software product.

### The Core Challenge — Three Tiers of Feasibility

Not all revenue streams are equally capturable. The platform must be honest with itself about which tier each stream falls into and build toward total capture deliberately rather than promising it before it exists.

**Tier 1 — Fully in the system from day one**
These are transactions that originate inside the platform. The money never touches an outside system. Full real-time tracking and public display is achievable immediately.

| Revenue Stream | Why It's Capturable |
|---|---|
| Pledge raise transactions | Platform-native payment |
| Investment round purchases | Platform-native payment |
| Beat marketplace sales | Platform-native payment |
| Fan tips | Platform-native payment |
| Merch sold through platform store | Platform fulfillment, platform payment |
| POS app sales at shows | Artist uses platform app, Stripe Connect handles payment |
| Ad revenue distributions | Calculated and distributed internally |
| Influencer attribution earnings | Tracked and paid internally |

**Tier 2 — Capturable via API integration, with some delay**
These streams originate outside the platform but can be pulled in via authenticated connections. True real-time is not possible because the upstream source pays on its own schedule — but daily estimates from API data can be displayed with actuals reconciled when payments arrive.

| Revenue Stream | Delay | How It's Captured |
|---|---|---|
| Streaming earnings | Monthly payout, daily estimate | Distribution partner API (DistroKid/TuneCore) + direct Spotify for Artists, Apple Music for Artists connections |
| YouTube Content ID earnings | Monthly | YouTube Studio API via auth connection |
| PRO performance earnings | Quarterly | ASCAP/BMI portal connection |
| SoundExchange earnings | Quarterly | SoundExchange portal connection |
| MLC mechanical earnings | Monthly/quarterly | MLC portal connection |
| Bandcamp direct sales | Real-time via API | Bandcamp API via auth connection |
| Ticket sales (Eventbrite, DICE, etc.) | Near real-time | Ticketing platform API integration |

The dashboard shows these as two numbers simultaneously: **estimated** (updated daily from API data) and **confirmed** (updated when actual payments clear). Both are public. The difference between them is the pipeline — money that has been earned but not yet paid by the upstream source.

**Tier 3 — Requires the platform to become the contracting entity**
These streams are where the real integrity risk lives. The money currently goes directly to the artist or band — bypassing the system entirely. Capturing them requires the platform to insert itself as the party the outside world pays, then distribute inward.

| Revenue Stream | Current Risk | Required Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Venue performance guarantees | Venue pays artist directly in cash or ACH | Platform invoices the venue; venue pays the platform; platform distributes |
| Show door splits | Cash at the door, artist counts it | POS app handles all door transactions; venue registered in platform |
| Sync licensing fees | Deals made offline, fees paid to whoever negotiated | Platform is the sync licensing agent; all inquiries route through platform |
| Brand deal payments | Handshake deals, outside payment | Platform contracts with brands on artist's behalf above defined threshold |
| Session work fees | Outside invoices, personal payment | Required to be logged in platform if performed under the artist entity |

Tier 3 is the hardest and most important. It requires the platform to do something traditional software doesn't do: become the legal counterparty in real-world deals. The venue doesn't just connect to an API — it receives an invoice from SoundSteak LLC and pays SoundSteak LLC. This requires the platform to be a licensed money transmitter or to operate through a regulated payment partner who handles that status.

### The Money Movement Infrastructure

**Stripe Connect** is the most likely backbone for day one — it handles multi-party payments, marketplace splits, automatic distribution to multiple recipients, and has the regulatory framework for money transmission built in. It handles both platform-native transactions (Tier 1) and incoming payments from outside parties (Tier 3, once the platform is the contracting entity).

Every participating entity — artists, investors, professionals, promoters — has a Stripe Connect account linked to their platform profile. When money enters, it routes to the correct accounts automatically based on the documented ownership split. No human touches it. No one decides who gets what. The ownership table is the instruction set.

### What Real-Time Actually Means

The platform is not responsible for how fast an upstream source pays. Spotify pays monthly. PROs pay quarterly. That is their schedule and it cannot be changed. What the platform is responsible for is what happens the instant that money arrives.

The rule is simple: **the moment money hits the platform, it is live. Publicly. Immediately.**

No batching. No holding. No internal processing delay before the dashboard updates. The second a PRO payment clears, every SteakHolder with a stake in that recording sees the number change. The second a streaming payment arrives, the artist's dashboard updates and every investor's earnings reflect it in real time.

The platform does not control when upstream sources pay. It controls everything that happens after. That window — from receipt to public display to automatic distribution — is where the platform's commitment to transparency lives, and it must be as close to zero as technology allows.

**What the public dashboard shows for every artist:**
- **Incoming** — money received today, this week, this month, broken down by source
- **Distributed** — how that money was split and to whom, the moment it was processed
- **Pipeline** — earnings confirmed upstream but not yet received, with expected settlement dates pulled from each source's known payment schedule
- **Lifetime** — total earnings across every stream since signing, always current

Every number is public. Every source is labeled. The pipeline is not a hidden backstage number — it is displayed prominently, because investors watching an artist with $4,000 in confirmed pipeline earnings have information worth having.

Upstream payment timing is a fact of the industry. Hiding the gap between earned and received is a choice. SoundSteak does not make that choice.

### The Venue Problem — The Hardest Piece

Venues have been paying bands in cash at the end of the night since music venues existed. Getting that money into the system requires either:

**Option A — Platform becomes the contracting entity**
The platform books the show. The venue signs a contract with SoundSteak. The venue pays SoundSteak. SoundSteak distributes. This requires the platform to have a booking function and legal standing as a contracting party. Big lift — but the right long-term answer.

**Option B — Venue registration**
Venues register on the platform and agree to pay through the system as a condition of booking signed artists. Registered venues receive a preferred partner badge and visibility to every cohort of funded artists looking for show opportunities. The carrot: access to a pipeline of investment-backed artists with active fanbases. The stick: signed artists are contractually required to only accept show guarantees from registered venues or to log outside payments through the POS app.

**Option C — Artist logging with audit capability**
For unregistered venues, the artist logs the received payment through the POS app immediately after receiving it. The system records the amount, the date, the venue, and the artist's confirmation. Not as clean as Options A or B, but creates a documented record and establishes a paper trail. Gaps between reported show income and expected show income based on ticket sales data can be flagged automatically.

The path is Option C on day one, Option B as the venue network grows, Option A as the platform's scale and legal infrastructure supports it.

### Public Display — What Transparency Actually Looks Like

Every artist's earnings dashboard is public. Not just visible to investors — visible to anyone. This is radical transparency made concrete.

What the public sees on any artist's profile:
- Total lifetime earnings, broken down by stream
- Current month earnings, updating daily
- Pipeline earnings — confirmed but not yet paid
- Earnings per investor tier — what founding equity holders have received vs. product investors
- Every transaction above a defined threshold, timestamped and categorized
- The ownership table — exactly who holds what percentage and what they've received

This is not standard practice anywhere in the music industry. Labels guard this information. Artists often don't even know their own numbers. SoundSteak makes it the default and makes opacity the exception that requires justification.

## Revenue Integrity & Transaction Control

Every dollar an artist earns in their capacity as a signed SoundSteak artist flows through the system. No exceptions. This is not punitive — it is the deal. The band agreement signed at onboarding makes this explicit. The system protects artists from each other, protects investors from artists, and protects the platform from disputes.

### The Core Problem — The Gray Area
The most common integrity failure: an artist has a friend make shirts, sells them at shows, pockets the cash, and investors never see a penny of that revenue. This is not hypothetical — it is what bands do constantly. Left unresolved it destroys investor trust and makes the platform's ownership model meaningless.

### The Solution — Platform as Merch Provider
The platform is the default and required merch provider. Not optionally. Every unit is accounted for at the point of order — before the shirts leave the warehouse. Investors are covered before the product exists.

- Artist designs products inside the platform
- Orders at wholesale / platform cost price
- Receives inventory, sells at shows at their chosen price
- Margin between cost and sell price flows through the system and distributes automatically
- Pre-funded merch runs — investment raise includes a merch budget line; investors fund the first run, earnings distribute back as units sell

The band agreement explicitly prohibits unlicensed merch produced outside the platform for any signed project.

### The Point-of-Sale App
Every sale at every show must run through the system. The platform provides a dedicated **SoundSteak POS app** on the artist's phone. Every transaction — card or logged cash — runs through it. The app knows what was sold, at what price, calculates every stakeholder's cut in real time, and distributes automatically.

This is not a burden. It is easier than a personal Square account because the artist never thinks about splitting money afterward. It just happens.

The POS app is also a point-of-presence for the platform at every local show, every pop-up, every market. Anywhere the artist sells anything, the platform is there. That is reach no streaming deal provides.

### Revenue Stream Accountability

| Revenue Stream | Gray Area Risk | Platform Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Streaming | Low — tracked by ISRC | Direct API ingestion |
| Merch — platform store | None | Default fulfillment system |
| Merch — made outside platform | High | Band agreement prohibits unlicensed merch; platform is sole merch provider |
| Ticket sales | Medium | Platform POS app; integrated ticketing required |
| Live show guarantees | High — cash from venues | Venue must be platform-registered or payment logged through app |
| Sync licensing | Medium — deals made offline | All sync inquiries routed through platform; platform negotiates or approves |
| Brand deals | High — handshake agreements | Band agreement requires all deals above a threshold run through platform |
| YouTube AdSense | Low | Auth connection handles it |
| Fan tips / direct support | Medium — Venmo, cash | Platform provides tipping mechanism; external tips must be logged |
| Crowdfunding outside platform | High | Band agreement prohibits external crowdfunding for signed projects |
| Session work | Gray | Separate from signed artist revenue unless performed under artist entity |

### New Possibilities This Opens
- **Pre-funded merch** — investors fund the inventory run, artist sells it, earnings distribute back automatically
- **Show advance system** — platform advances touring costs against projected ticket earnings
- **Sync marketplace** — music supervisors browse platform catalog, licensing deals close inside the system, fees distribute automatically
- **Fan tipping** — a SoundSteak tip at a show goes through the system like any other transaction
- **Venue integration** — registered venues route show guarantee payments automatically
- **Wholesale purchasing** — artist buys merch at platform cost, sells at their own markup, margin distributes through system

## Platform Integrations & Auth Access

Connecting external accounts is **required at signing — not optional.** Optional connections produce incomplete data, incomplete dashboards, and a broken investor experience. Full connectivity is a condition of being on the platform.

### The Auth Approach
- **OAuth connections** where available — artist logs into each platform through SoundSteak and grants read access. Platform never stores passwords, only access tokens. Secure, standard, industry normal.
- **Guided import flows** where OAuth isn't available — upload a statement, connect via API key, or grant CSV export access.
- **Read access only** for data ingestion. Write access only where needed (distributing a release, registering metadata). Clear disclosure to the artist of exactly what each connection can and cannot do. Trust is the whole game — artists see precisely what they're granting.

### Required Connections at Signing

**Streaming Platforms**
- Spotify for Artists — streams, listeners, saves, playlist adds, geographic data
- Apple Music for Artists — second largest platform, different audience demographic
- YouTube Studio — views, watch time, subscriber growth, Content ID earnings
- SoundCloud — relevant for certain genres and emerging artists
- Amazon Music for Artists — growing platform, frequently overlooked

**Social Platforms**
- TikTok — most important discovery platform currently; video views, follower growth, sound usage by other creators
- Instagram — follower count, post engagement, reel reach
- YouTube — social metrics tracked separately from music metrics
- Facebook — event promotion, older demographic reach
- X / Twitter — engagement, mentions, conversation volume around the artist

**Distribution & Earnings**
- DistroKid / TuneCore / CD Baby — wherever the artist currently distributes; earnings statements pull automatically
- PRO portal (ASCAP or BMI) — performance earnings pulled directly
- SoundExchange — digital performance earnings
- MLC portal — mechanical earnings

**Additional**
- Bandcamp — direct sales, merch revenue, fan data
- Songkick / Bandsintown — live show data, ticket sales, attendance
- Merchandise platforms — Printful, Shopify, Merch by Amazon, etc.

### Why This Must Be Required
- Optional connections produce incomplete dashboards — investors lose confidence
- Incomplete data breaks the core value proposition for all three audiences
- Artists who don't connect their accounts can't prove their numbers — weakening their pledge raise
- The platform's real-time analytics are only as good as the data feeding them
- Consistency across all artist profiles makes the platform trustworthy as a data source

## The Music Player — A First-Class Experience

SoundSteak is not a platform that happens to have a music player. It is a music platform whose player is the emotional center of the entire experience. Every other feature — the investment dashboard, the Cues, the badge system, the journey tracker — exists in orbit around the moment a person presses play.

The player must be the best music player available. Not competitive with Spotify. Better than Spotify. It has to earn the right to be someone's primary listening experience — because if it does, every other layer of the platform becomes part of their daily life instead of something they visit occasionally.

### What Other Players Don't Do

Other players know what you listened to. SoundSteak knows what you own, what you earned, who you influenced, and what your listening means to the artist playing in your ears right now.

When you stream a song you have a stake in, the player knows. When the song hits a streaming milestone while you're listening, the player knows. When the artist you backed in last year's cohort releases their single, the player is where you hear it first — and where your earnings from that release begin.

No other music player can show you that. Because no other music player has that data.

### What the Player Plays

The SoundSteak player plays SoundSteak releases only. Exclusively. Every track in the player is contracted, verified, investor-backed, and properly registered. There is no open submissions path. There is no streaming platform shortcut. The music that plays in this player went through the event, the contract, the LLC, the raise, and the year of work.

That exclusivity is not a limitation — it is the signal. When something plays in this player, every listener already knows: someone believed in this enough to fund it before it existed. That context is embedded in every single play.

Scarcity of catalog is a feature. A small library of music that every listener knows is real is more valuable than an unlimited library of music that means nothing.

### Principles for the Player Experience
- Feels like a music player first — not a financial dashboard with audio attached
- Investment and earnings data surfaces contextually, not constantly — it enriches the listening experience without overwhelming it
- The artist's journey is visible but ambient — a listener knows where their artist is without having to navigate away from the music
- Discovery is built into the experience — the cycle model means there is always a class of new artists at an early stage worth finding
- Social listening has financial texture — sharing a song you own a stake in is a different act than sharing one you don't, and the player acknowledges that

### The Share — Story and Song Together
When someone shares a song on SoundSteak, the share carries their story with it automatically. Not as an optional caption. Not as a separate step. The moment you share, your connection to that song is part of what gets sent.

The share object might show:
- The song, playable directly from the share
- "I've been a SteakHolder since day one" — or whatever their actual status is
- How early they were — "I pledged before they had 500 followers"
- What the song has earned for them, if they choose to show it
- A single line they write themselves — because their own words are always the most powerful

The person receiving the share doesn't just get a link. They get a recommendation from someone with skin in the game, with a documented relationship to the music, with a real reason to believe in it. That is categorically more compelling than a Spotify link with a screenshot.

And because the share is coming from inside the platform, clicking it brings the recipient into the SoundSteak experience — where they can listen, see the artist's journey, and become a SteakHolder themselves in the same session.

The share is the flywheel's most powerful single mechanism. It needs to be effortless, beautiful, and carry the full weight of the sharer's story every time.

### Things to Watch For as Data Flows
These are not features yet — they are signals to stay alert to as the platform accumulates real data. The right opportunities will reveal themselves when the data exists to support them.

- **The moment a listener's stream tips an artist over a milestone** — the 10,000th stream, the 100,000th. The person whose play crossed the threshold deserves to know. That is a story no other platform can tell.
- **Earnings in real time while listening** — watching a fraction of a cent accumulate as you stream a recording you invested in is a novel experience nobody has had. Whether that's delightful or distracting depends on how it's surfaced. Worth exploring carefully.
- **The early believer data** — you listened to this artist 340 times before they had 1,000 followers. That is a credential. A story. A badge moment.
- **The influence chain** — your share brought someone in who pledged, who told three people, two of whom became investors. The full downstream effect of a single share, visualized.
- **Cohort listening** — a curated experience of listening to an entire class of artists as a collection, the way you'd listen to a compilation album. The Class of 2027, front to back.
- **The journey soundtrack** — a playlist that follows an artist's development in real time, automatically updating as they hit milestones and release new material.
